Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Rancho Cucamonga

Think of drywall as a wick. When water reaches the bottom of a wall, the gypsum and paper draw it upward, often a foot or more above the level the water ever reached on the floor. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

What It Looks Like

  • Fasteners and joint lines becoming visible on a once smooth wall
  • Flooring along the wall that is damp, stained or starting to lift
  • Mildew smell you notice most when standing close to one section of wall
  • Discolored lower drywall with a visible line marking how high water wicked
  • Speckled growth showing up where furniture sits against the wall or at corners
  • Trim along the floor that has puffed up, twisted or come loose
Save or Replace

What Stays and What Goes

It depends on the material, how long it stayed wet and how clean the water was. We test before we decide, and we tell you why.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Map the Moisture

Using meter readings and thermal images, our team outlines where the water went, upward and sideways, on both faces of the wall and in neighboring rooms.

2

Sort Save From Remove

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Cut or Ventilate

For walls being saved, baseboards come off and air is directed into the cavity. Walls that cannot be kept get a flood cut along a level line, with the wet insulation pulled.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.

If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Wicking Begins

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

Hours 24 to 48

Swelling and Blisters

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Growth is able to get going on the paper that lines the hidden side.

Days 3 through 7

Panels Lose Strength

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. Insulation slumps inside the wall, odor builds and mold spreads across the hidden back side.

Past one week

Damage Reaches the Studs

Studs and plates stay wet long enough to grow mold and begin to decay. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Water Next Door

Water passes under the bottom plate into the next room or closet. Every wall involved is tested on its two faces, and so is the adjacent flooring.

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Batts of fiberglass absorb water and keep it pressed to the framing. No amount of airflow on the painted side will dry a cavity packed with wet batts.

Walls You Cannot See

Sections of wall covered by cabinets, a vanity or shelving stay damp because air never reaches them. Checking under the cabinet base and on the reverse face tells us if it is wet.

Mold Inside Cavities

The back of the drywall is dark, still and lined with paper, which is ideal for mold. It can cover the hidden side while the painted face looks clean.
